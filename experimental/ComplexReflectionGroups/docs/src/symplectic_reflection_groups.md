# [Symplectic reflection groups](@id srg-manual)

!!! note "Preliminary support"
    General symplectic reflection groups, their classification, and their reflection
    data are not yet implemented in this component. The currently available
    construction is the cotangent doubling of a known complex reflection group,
    documented below. It is one family of symplectic reflection groups, not a
    constructor for all of them.

## Symplectic spaces and reflections

A **symplectic form** on a complex vector space $U$ is a nondegenerate alternating
bilinear form $\omega:U\times U\to\mathbb C$. In particular, $\dim U$ is even. The
symplectic group $\mathrm{Sp}(U,\omega)$ consists of transformations preserving
$\omega$.

A **symplectic reflection** is an element $s$ with
$\operatorname{rank}(1-s)=2$ in a finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Sp}(U,\omega)$. A
**symplectic reflection group** is a finite subgroup generated by such elements
[EG02](@cite). Unlike a complex reflection, a symplectic reflection fixes a
codimension-two space. Its geometry is therefore not described by the reflecting
hyperplanes and cyclic pointwise stabilizers of the complex case.

## Cotangent doubling

For a vector space $V$ with dual $V^*$, the space $U=V\oplus V^*$ has a natural
symplectic form

```math
\omega\bigl((v,f),(w,h)\bigr)=h(v)-f(w).
```

In a basis of $V$ and its dual basis the Gram matrix is

```math
\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix}.
```

The action of $A\in\mathrm{GL}(V)$ on the dual is $A^{-\mathsf T}$, so the
**cotangent doubling** acts on $U$ by

```math
A^\star=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\0&A^{-\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}.
```

With OSCAR's row-action convention, preservation of the form means
$A^\star\Omega(A^\star)^{\mathsf T}=\Omega$. Moreover,

```math
\operatorname{rank}(1-A^\star)=2\operatorname{rank}(1-A).
```

Thus a complex reflection doubles to a symplectic reflection, and a finite complex
reflection group doubles to a symplectic reflection group of the same order.

For example, doubling the two-dimensional group $G_4$ gives a four-dimensional
symplectic reflection group:

```jldoctest
julia> W = complex_reflection_group(4, :CHEVIE);

julia> G = symplectic_reflection_group(W);

julia> (degree(G), order(G), is_symplectic_reflection_group(G))
(4, 24, true)

julia> A = matrix(gens(G)[1]);

julia> rank(identity_matrix(base_ring(G), degree(G)) - A)
2

julia> Omega = gram_matrix(symplectic_form(G));

julia> A * Omega * transpose(A) == Omega
true
```

[`symplectic_doubling`](@ref) constructs the cotangent representation of a finite
matrix group. [`symplectic_reflection_group`](@ref) applies it to a known complex
reflection group and marks the result as a symplectic reflection group. No recognition
of arbitrary symplectic matrix groups is performed.

### Source data and the two summands

The doubling retains the source group, the alternating form, and the row-space
inclusions of $V$ and $V^*$. If the source has a known complex reflection group type
and model, these are retained as **source metadata**, not assigned as a complex
reflection group type of the doubled representation:

```jldoctest
julia> W = complex_reflection_group(4, :CHEVIE);

julia> G = symplectic_reflection_group(W);

julia> symplectic_doubling_source(G) === W
true

julia> symplectic_doubling_source_type(G) == complex_reflection_group_type(W)
true

julia> primal, dual = symplectic_doubling_block_embeddings(G);

julia> (size(primal), size(dual))
((2, 4), (2, 4))

julia> complex_reflection_group_type(G) === nothing
true
```

The construction also includes trivial groups. The trivial reflection group on a
line doubles to the trivial group on a symplectic plane, still with no generators:

```jldoctest
julia> Z = complex_reflection_group(complex_reflection_group_type(1, 1, 1));

julia> G = symplectic_reflection_group(Z);

julia> (degree(G), order(G), isempty(gens(G)))
(2, 1, true)
```
