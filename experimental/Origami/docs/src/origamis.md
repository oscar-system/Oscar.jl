# Origamis

An origami of degree $d$ is a connected covering $p\colon X^* \to E^*$ of degree $d$ of the once-punctured
torus $E^*$, considered up to isomorphism of coverings. This page describes how to construct origamis, how to
compute their invariants, and how the group ${\rm SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ acts on them. The last section is devoted
to a special family of origamis, the cyclic torus covers.

## Basic construction of origamis

In OSCAR, an origami is represented by the monodromy of the covering: lifting a horizontal and a vertical closed
curve, which generate the fundamental group of $E^*$, permutes the $d$ squares of $X^*$. These are the
permutations $h = \sigma_x$ and $v = \sigma_y$ from the introduction, and the group they generate is the
monodromy group returned by `perm_group`. The function `origami` checks that this group acts transitively unless
`check=false` is given, and `are_equivalent` decides whether two origamis describe isomorphic coverings.

```@docs
origami(h::PermGroupElem, v::PermGroupElem; check=true)
horizontal_perm(o::Origami)
vertical_perm(o::Origami)
degree(o::Origami)
perm_group(o::Origami)
are_equivalent(o1::Origami, o2::Origami)
```

## Computing attributes of origamis

Gluing the squares produces a closed translation surface. At most of the corners of the squares the
total angle is $2\pi$, but at finitely many points, the singularities, it is a larger multiple of $2\pi$. The
functions in this section compute invariants of this surface: its stratum (the degrees of the singularities)
and genus, the index of its monodromy group, the sum of its Lyapunov exponents, its automorphisms (translations
and point reflections), whether it is hyperelliptic, and its decomposition into maximal horizontal cylinders.

```@docs
stratum(o::Origami)
genus(o::Origami)
index_monodromy_group(o::Origami)
sum_of_lyapunov_exponents(o::Origami)
translations(o::Origami)
point_reflections(o::Origami)
is_hyperelliptic(o::Origami)
cylinder_structure(o::Origami)
```

## Normal forms of origamis

The function `normal_form` computes the canonical representative mentioned in the introduction, so that two
origamis are equivalent if and only if their normal forms coincide.

```@docs
normal_form(o::Origami)
```

## ${\rm SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ actions on origamis

A matrix $A \in {\rm SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ acts on an origami by applying the linear map $A$ to the translation surface
and cutting the result back into unit squares. This defines an action of ${\rm SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ on
equivalence classes of origamis, and the stabilizer of an origami is its Veech group. Since
${\rm SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is generated by
$S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (rotation by $\pi/2$) and
$T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ (horizontal shear), there are dedicated functions for
these generators and their inverses, and `action_sl2` computes the action of an arbitrary matrix by writing it
as a word in $S$ and $T$.

```@docs
action_s(o::Origami)
action_t(o::Origami)
action_s_inv(o::Origami)
action_t_inv(o::Origami)
action_sl2(A::ZZMatrix, o::Origami)
```

## Cyclic torus covers

Let $T_n$ be the torus built from $n \times n$ squares, punctured at the $n^2$ vertices of the squares. A cyclic
torus cover of degree $d$ is an origami that arises as a cyclic covering of $T_n$ of degree $d$: we take $d$
copies of $T_n$ and glue them along slits on the edges of the squares. Such a cover is determined by its
monodromy vector, which assigns to each element of a basis of the first homology of $T_n$ an element of
$\mathbb{Z} / d\mathbb{Z}$.

### Construction

These functions construct cyclic torus covers, either directly from the slits or from a monodromy vector with
respect to one of the two bases $S$ and $L$ of the homology of $T_n$. The function `base_change_l_to_s`
converts monodromy vectors between these two bases. The comb origamis of [HS07](@cite) are a special case of
degree $2$.

```@docs
generalized_cyclic_torus_cover(n::Int, d::Int, vslits::Vector{Int64}, hslits::Vector{Int64})
comb_origami(n::Int, x::Int, y::Int)
cyclic_torus_cover_origamiS(n::Int, d::Int, v::Vector{Int64})
cyclic_torus_cover_origamiL(n::Int, d::Int, v::Vector{Int64})
base_change_l_to_s(n::Int)
```

### Actions on the homology of $T_n$

The basis $L$ of the homology of $T_n$ consists of a horizontal closed curve $a$, a vertical closed curve $b$,
and the loops around $n^2 - 1$ of the $n^2$ punctures; the loop around the last puncture is not needed, since
the sum of all puncture loops is zero. Affine maps of $T_n$ that fix the lower left corner, as well as
translations of $T_n$, act on this homology. The functions in this section return these actions as integer
matrices with respect to $L$, acting on monodromy vectors.

```@docs
translation_group_on_homology_of_tn(n::Int)
action_of_t_on_homology_of_tn(n::Int)
action_of_s_on_homology_of_tn(n::Int)
action_of_matrix_on_homology_of_tn(n::Int, A::Matrix)
```

### Actions on the extended homology of $T_n$

The extended homology adds the loop around the $n^2$-th puncture to the basis $L$. The actions on it are
obtained from the actions on the homology of $T_n$: the additional loop is first mapped to minus the sum of all
other puncture loops, and the result has no component along the additional loop. As a consequence, products of
these matrices correspond to products in ${\rm SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, but the matrices are not invertible.

```@docs
action_of_s_on_extended_homology_of_tn(n::Int)
action_of_t_on_extended_homology_of_tn(n::Int)
action_of_matrix_on_extended_homology_of_tn(n::Int, A::Matrix)
```
